# a re-worded unverifiable claim is asked about instead of quietly costing the round again

An obligation's key is content-addressed over the claim's own words. `_claim_norm` absorbs
case, whitespace and a trailing full stop and says plainly that it absorbs nothing else, so a
judge that says the same thing differently next round mints a different key and the
acknowledgement a human already gave does not carry. Observed live: an orchestrator settled
two unverifiable claims with `--acknowledge`, the next round's judge re-worded both, and the
same two questions cost round 2 its confidence — with nothing in the report connecting the
acknowledgement that named nothing to the claims that had just arrived.

Both halves of that were already emitted and were reported apart. They are now paired: a round
where an `--acknowledge` names no claim it raised **and** a claim arrives that no
acknowledgement names prints one question naming both keys — are these the same claim under
new keys?

It is a question and not a match. Nothing compares the two claims' wording, which is the
property `claim_key` exists to protect; the round's confidence is not bought back by asking,
and the caller either re-acknowledges under the new key or does not. The pairing fires on a key
passed on this round's command line, never on one inherited from an earlier round — an
inherited acknowledgement names no claim this round in the ordinary case where it worked, so
pairing it with whatever unrelated obligation turned up would put the question again every
round for the rest of the cycle.

The durable version is to key an obligation on the capability limit that could not be checked
rather than on the judge's sentence about it. The judge authors the sentence and does not
author the limit, which is what makes one a stable id and the other an unstable one. That
belongs with #623's typed obligations.

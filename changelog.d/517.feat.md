# a seat can be parked out of sight and brought back, and its agent never knows

A screen is a fixed amount of glass, and the only way to free some was `x`, which ends the
agent, its lease and its claims. A `–` on every seat's cell on the bar, or `C-q p`, now parks the seat you are in: its pane leaves the
row, the row closes over it, and the agent keeps running — same process, same context, same
board presence. `C-q u` brings back the one parked last, and `C-q 1`-`9` or a click on its
dim `◌ seat N` cell on the bar brings back a particular one. Nothing here calls `qb-end` or
tells the board; parked is a property of one screen.

### What it does about the parts that are not the tape's

A seat has no canonical place to return to, so the pane next to it is recorded and it comes
back beside that one, ordered by seat number inside the row. The bar's cells are generated
from the current window, so parked seats are drawn from a session option instead. The last
visible seat is refused — `t` and `d` rejoin beside a seat and need one. Park and unpark
take a per-screen `flock` so two presses cannot interleave (#457 is still open for the
tape and dash).

A seat alone in its row comes back splitting one cell of the other row, not as a full-width
row. A screen built with an older `qb-seat-key` still does nothing on `p` or `u` (#484).

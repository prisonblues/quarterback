# round N now asks the seats round N-1 did not, and a seat it does not ask is never counted as coverage

Every round dispatched the same panel. A seat that read the diff in round 1 read the fix in
round 2 with its own round-1 findings in front of it, and the cheapest thing it could produce
was more of the same shape. Nothing biased a later round toward the questions nobody had asked
yet.

`panel_seats.route_seats` is the rule that does: round N's seats are the **set difference**
against round N-1's, spent within #776's per-round budget, falling back to repeating a prior
seat only when the complement runs out. It is the division of labour `round_budgets` already
declared — the multiplier says *how many* seats a round dispatches, floored at 1, and the
routing says *which*.

### Which seats a round asked is now recorded, apart from which it was configured with

`reviewers_selected` says what `.harness-rules` and `--reviewers` asked for. The payload's new
`seat_routing` block says what the round then actually put a question to: `dispatched`, `held`,
the budget it spent, the round its complement was taken against, and **one word per selected
seat saying why** — from a closed vocabulary, so a seat's silence always has a name. A skipped
seat's reason is as informative as a selected one's, and it is the half a reader six weeks later
cannot reconstruct: a seat absent from `reviewers` looks exactly like a seat nobody configured.

### The fifth reason a seat has no row

This is the half that makes the routing safe rather than a filter, and it is why #776 declined
to cut the seat set at all. Four reasons a seat could have no row were already distinguishable —
not installed, ran and failed, ran and was silent, cut by a ceiling. A seat that is installed,
selected and then *not asked* is a fifth, and without it that seat's silence is indistinguishable
from a seat that ran and found nothing: the next round banks *"four seats looked and three had
nothing to say"* when three were never asked.

Two readers learned it, and each failed differently:

- **`coverage_veto`** gives a held seat its own line, worded so it is neither coverage nor a
  fault in the seat — nothing failed, nothing timed out, nothing was missing from the box. It is
  a **veto**, deliberately: a round that did not ask every seat it could have asked is not
  evidence that the PR is clean, so **a cycle that wants a confident stop has to end on a round
  that asks everybody.** That is what "never a dropped seat counted as coverage" costs when it is
  meant. It is not the standing veto the absent-CLI and code-blind exemptions rule out — those
  are facts about the host and the panel's design that no round can change, while this is false
  on every round of every repo running the shipped flat curve and answerable by asking
  everybody.
- **`load_baseline`** refuses to let a partially-dispatched round be the one that erases every
  earlier round's coverage gap. That round satisfies every other term of `reread` — its seats
  ran, read their whole target and read a diff — so it would have closed gaps it never looked
  at, which is the same defect arriving at the one reader with the power to delete the evidence.

### Two floors, taken out of the budget before the complement gets any of it

A seat that can read the code (`SEAT_READS_CODE`) is kept whenever the round has one to keep: it
is the only dimension this code can actually name, and a round that routed away its last pair of
eyes able to open a file would turn *"I could not read the caller"* from a finding into the
panel's shape. And at least one seat, always — a round with no seats reviewed nothing, and a
budget able to manufacture a clean round is the one thing the curve must never be. A seat this
box cannot run is asked anyway and does not spend a slot: it costs nothing, and the `absent` row
it writes is load-bearing in both readers above.

### Unknown is not "nobody"

*The prior round dispatched nobody* and *no prior round said what it dispatched* are different
facts, and the second is the common one — round 1, a standalone `/panel`, every payload written
before this existed. Unknown means **no cut**: every selected seat is dispatched, exactly as
before, whatever the curve says. A budget with no recorded prior set has no order to spend
itself in but the order the vendor names happen to be written in, and choosing seats by alphabet
is the one thing #790 refused. So the routing fails toward reading *more*, never less.

### Inert on the shipped fleet

`round_budgets.multipliers` ships `[1.0]`, so no budget is computed at all and every selected
seat is asked — with `all` recorded against it, so a reader can still tell "routing ran and
decided nothing" from "this payload predates routing". A repo that has opted into a curve gets
the seat set scaled from the round it first has a dispatch record to complement, and gets a
config note and a report line naming the budget, the round the complement was taken against, and
every seat it did not ask.

### Two of the block's six keys are columns, and the other four are not

`review_runs.seats_dispatched` and `seats_held` keep the lengths of the two lists.
`reviewers_selected` is what the round was **configured** with and now answers a different question
from what it asked; nothing on this board held the second, and nothing else can rebuild it — a held
seat's `review_reviewers` row says `ran: false`, and so does a missing CLI, a crash and a ceiling
cut. The word that separates those four is `seat_routing.why`, which is not stored.

They are columns because the question they are owed is the one nobody can ask later: **across
hundreds of rounds, does a round that asked fewer seats find less?** `multipliers` ships `[1.0]`
precisely because nobody has measured a curve, and the payload the answer would be computed from
lives in a temp directory on whichever host ran the panel. Both, because neither half is the
measurement — three seats out of three and three out of six are the same dispatched count. Each
carries a `>= 0` CHECK, on `review_runs`' standing rule that the API is not the only writer.

`why`, `budget`, `prior_round` and `prior_dispatched` get no column. They are that round's working,
already in the payload it published, and `GET /review/findings` lays a cycle's rounds out in order:
`why` is an unbounded object per run, `budget` is arithmetic derivable two ways with two chances to
disagree, and the prior pair is this board's own row one round earlier.
`tests/test_payload_key_drift.py` carries that decision in writing, which is the only other way past
the drift check — the same split #770's `fix_blast_lane` and #771's repeat counts made one release
ago.

**A silence is not a zero.** NULL is a round that made no routing decision: a title skip, a
pre-flight refusal, a spend-ceiling refusal, or a producer older than the block. `seats_held: 0` is
a measurement — this round asked every seat it could — and it is what every round on the shipped
flat curve records. Folded together, the baseline the first repo to opt into a curve gets compared
against would be built out of rounds that never routed.

Both counts ride `GET /reviews`, `GET /review/{id}` and `GET /review/findings` — the reader is a
correlation over a population, not a person opening one round — and `GET /review/convergence` grows
a `seat_routing` block: the runs that answered, the seats dispatched and held across the window,
how many rounds held anything back, and the held share. It is counted per run and moves no
convergence bucket.

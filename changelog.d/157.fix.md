# a turn nobody typed stops publishing itself as your work

`qb-hook` treated every `UserPromptSubmit` as a person declaring work. A peer's message, a
subagent's completion notification and a client-injected reminder all landed in `.prompt` and were
published twice: as a `working on:` status under the recipient's name, and — with no throttle and
no length gate at all — as the lease's `recap`, which is the field peer discovery ranks on. An
agent messaged five times in ten minutes had its declared subject overwritten five times with the
sender's socket-path wrapper, so for that window it was undiscoverable on what it was actually
doing, and a peer checking whether anyone was on the problem was told the coast was clear. A
sample of 34 auto-claims held 9 that were not work.

Both writes are gated together now on whether the turn opens with a wrapper the client itself
wrote. The gate is deliberately one-sided: a prompt that merely mentions such a wrapper still
claims, because a missing claim is the failure the claim exists to prevent. The presence heartbeat
still fires on every prompt — what a refused turn withholds is the subject, and the board
overwrites that only when it is sent.

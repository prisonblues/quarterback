# a turn nobody typed stops publishing itself as your work

`qb-hook` treated every `UserPromptSubmit` as a person declaring work. A peer's message, a
subagent's completion notification and a client-injected reminder all landed in `.prompt` and were
published twice: as a `working on:` status under the recipient's name, and — with no throttle and
no length gate at all — as the lease's `recap`, which is the field peer discovery ranks on. A
sample of 34 auto-claims held 9 that were not work. Both writes now share one gate, and an injected
turn no longer spends the ten-minute claim throttle, which used to silence the genuine prompt
behind it.

The gate is deliberately one-sided: it refuses only a turn whose opening is a wrapper the client
itself wrote, so a prompt that merely mentions one still claims. A missing claim is the failure the
claim exists to prevent. The presence heartbeat still fires on every prompt — what a refused turn
withholds is the subject, and the board overwrites that only when it is sent.

Scope, since the entry would otherwise read as bigger than it is. A corrupted subject lasted until
the end of the turn rather than indefinitely: `Stop` leases again with a recap read from the typed
history, which never holds a wrapper, so the subject repaired itself one turn later. And the short
follow-up ("yes", "carry on") that no longer writes a subject here still reaches the lease from
that same `Stop` seconds afterwards — untouched on purpose, because what it ships is something a
person actually typed.

One consequence worth knowing: a session whose lease has expired (30 minutes idle) and whose next
turn is short or injected now comes back with no subject at all rather than a wrong one, and is
unranked by peer discovery until its next real prompt or `Stop`.

One source is untouched and tracked separately (#764): a scripted session whose prompt genuinely is
a system prompt carries no wrapper to recognise, so the panel seats still claim theirs. That is the
smaller half of what was measured, and it is the half only the harness that writes those prompts
can declare.

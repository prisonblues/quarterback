# a coverage declaration somebody answered can now be retired

A `could_not_assess` line vetoes the round's confident stop, and there was no way
to retire one after somebody answered it. `panel.py --acknowledge` takes `uc-`
claim keys only — the unverifiable-claims register — so a coverage declaration
was permanent for the life of that round's record whatever anybody subsequently
learned. `preland`'s `review` check reads `stop_confident` off the board row and
lists the vetoes verbatim, so an answered question went on holding the landing
exactly as hard as an unanswered one.

That is #716's shape one layer out. There, evidence a seat *could not gather* had
no channel into the round; here, evidence somebody *did* gather has no channel
back into it.

**Measured.** lexray#1631 round 2 stopped unearned on three vetoes. Two were
`could_not_assess` by the claude seat, which cannot execute anything (#92, and
reviewers stay non-executing — a permanent property of the design rather than a
gap). Both were closed from a worktree pinned at the PR head inside ten minutes:
one by reading the test file, one by three runs of the suite under three
different environments. `preland` reported both verbatim afterwards, and the PR
still read HOLD on them.

### `--assessed ca-xxxxxxxx:'<what you measured>'`

Every declaration this round is actually vetoing on now carries a key — `ca-`
and twelve hex, content-addressed over the declaration's own text the way an
obligation's key is over the claim's — and the report prints it beside the
declaration, exactly as claim keys are printed. `--assessed` records that
somebody answered it, and the register is inherited through `--baseline`, so it
is done once per cycle and not once per round.

**Per declaration, never in bulk.** #547's rule, for #547's reason: a blanket yes
is the cheap gate, and a gate that always passes is worse than one that always
holds because it looks like assurance. There is no flag that answers them all.

**It takes a note**, and unlike an acknowledgement that is most of the value.
An acknowledged claim records that somebody accepted a risk; an assessed
declaration records that somebody measured a fact, and *what* they measured is
the half a later reader can check.

### the two registers are disjoint, and each door refuses the other's keys

A declaration the judge ruled structurally unanswerable is an obligation and is
discharged at `--acknowledge`; everything else is a declaration and is answered
here. `reached_declarations` walks `coverage_veto`'s own seats under its own
recorded state, so a declaration answerable at both doors cannot exist — and a
blind seat's declarations, which cost the round nothing today, never acquire a
key that implies they do.

`--assessed` exempts the per-seat declaration lines and nothing else: not a
truncation, not an absent seat, not CI, not a judge skip. It cannot empty a veto
list except by answering it one line at a time, which is the work rather than a
way around it.

### self-grading, recorded rather than refused

The orchestrator marking its own round's vetoes closed is the actor attesting to
its own work. That is the objection `--escalated` already carries and
`record-outcome`'s `refuted` already answers: the loop takes the caller's word
and records the round, the note and `--assessed-by`'s claimed assessor so the
claim is checkable afterwards. Without `--assessed-by` the row is marked
**unattested** rather than refused (#40's rule), the report renders the split,
and the name is published as a claim — "rich says so", never "signed off by
rich" — because nothing here can authenticate a person.

That is strictly stronger than what it replaces, where the answer was recorded
nowhere at all and the veto stood on a question that had been settled.

### `preland` needed no change

The subtraction is in `coverage_veto`, so an assessed declaration never reaches
`round_stop.veto`, never reaches the board row's `stop_veto`, and is therefore
never listed by `preland`'s `review` check — which reads that field and nothing
else. One implementation of the rule, and no edit to the file #717 is separately
reworking.

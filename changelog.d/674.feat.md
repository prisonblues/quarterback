# a declination a human can retract, and the landing it gives back

#665 gave a fix pass a way to record a correction it could not make, so the next round inherits the
fact instead of paying to rediscover it. It gave nobody a way to say the correction has since been
made — and the cost of that is bigger than #665 wrote down.

### the chain, which was not in the original issue

A stopped round holding a declination appends a veto. `confident` requires an empty veto, so the
round reports `stop_confident: false`. And `preland`'s `_round_stop_earned` turns a false
`stop_confident` into a **failed check** rather than a warning under `--require-earned-stop` — the
mode `/panel-review-pr` §7 runs precisely when it is about to offer to land.

So one declination held a PR out of a strict landing **for the rest of the cycle**, with a fresh cycle
the only exit. And because a `--declined` key that names no finding is deliberately retained — dropping
it would recreate the failure that register exists to close — a **mistyped key did it just as
effectively as a real one**.

### `--retract KEY`

The fourth register of the shape `escalated` (#221), `acknowledged` (#547) and `declined` (#665)
already have, and the only one that CANCELS another. It takes a bare key, because a retraction has
nothing to say about why: the reason belongs to the declination it lifts, and the point is that the
declination is no longer owed.

Subtracted from the register **once**, before anything reads it. Every consequence of a declination —
`is_new` moving the key into `repeated`, `declined_outstanding`, the veto, and through it the
`preland` hold — hangs off one dictionary, so lifting the key there lifts all of them together and no
call site has to learn a second register exists.

Inherited through `--baseline` on `acknowledged`'s terms, and the earliest round that retracted owns
the date: a human act performed outside the loop that no later round makes untrue, and re-passing it
must not re-date it to now.

### why it is a human act and not an inference

Two tempting shortcuts, both refused. **A fix pass reporting that it fixed the thing** is the actor
attesting to its own work, which is the whole of #622. **A finding being absent from a later round**
is not evidence of a repair when that round's scope never re-read the file — the same reason #665
bounded its own register the way it did rather than to the current round's findings.

A bad key is refused rather than noted, on `--declined`'s rule and more sharply: a retraction that
matches nothing leaves the veto standing while its caller reads the silence as the PR having been
unblocked, and here that silence withholds a landing. A retraction that lifts nothing says so; one
that lifts something says that too.

# a correction the fixer could not make now survives the round that declared it

A fix pass that identifies a correction and cannot make it says so into the void. On a recent
cycle the fixer declared two corrections it could not pay for under the growth ceiling, the
declaration went nowhere, and the next round spent its own budget rediscovering one of them —
`classify()`'s now-wrong KEEP reason — and reported it as a fresh finding. The information
existed, the fixer was honest about it, and the loop threw it away.

`--declined <key>:<budget|premise|scope|refuted>` records it. It is the third register of the
shape `escalated` (#221) and `acknowledged` (#547) already have — a short, keyed,
round-authored record that travels on the `--baseline` payload — and the only one that carries
a reason as well as a key, because "priced out under a ceiling" and "I think this finding is
wrong" ask the round after for opposite things.

**It loosens nothing.** The finding stays outstanding, stays counted at every one of
`round_stop`'s four rules, stays a fix pass's work, and buys another round exactly as it did;
it is subtracted from no filter and appears in no rule. What it changes is what a round may
CLAIM. A finding matching an inherited declaration is reported `new_this_round: false` and
marked 🧾 with the round and the word, because an earlier round raised it and calling it a
fresh discovery overstates what this round found. And a cycle that ENDS with declarations on
the record takes a veto line and cannot report `converged: true` — it ran out of corrections
anybody was willing to make, which is not the same as running out of defects. That direction
is what makes the register safe to be written by the one actor it reports on: there is no
declaration a fixer can make that buys it a smaller round, a bigger budget or an easier stop.

The round that ends such a cycle posts one `needs-human` `decision` naming the keys, their
rounds and their reasons, so a PR that lands with known-unfixed defects lands with them named.

**Nothing retracts a declaration**, and the published `round_stop.declined_outstanding` is the
cycle's whole register rather than the subset this round raised. Bounding it the way
`escalated_outstanding` is bounded would silence it in exactly the case that motivated it: a
round under the default `increment` scope never re-reads the file the declined correction was
owed in. The cost is that a correction genuinely made later goes on costing the cycle its
`converged` until the cycle ends, which is the conservative direction — #617's `--new-cycle`
is the clean start for a PR that has had the work done.

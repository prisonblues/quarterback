# a push landing mid-round costs the round its confident stop

A panel round decides what to review against the head it opened with, and re-reads the head
after the diff has been fetched. When a push landed inside that window the later commit was
recorded — deliberately, because it is where the next round's fix range starts — and the
commits between the two heads were then the review target of no round at all: past the
increment this round read, before the increment the next one will read. The cycle could stop
with nothing new and nothing outstanding and stamp `converged: true` over code nobody had
looked at, which is a failure recording as a success.

A scoped round whose head moved now takes a veto line naming both commits and saying what
became unreviewable, so `confident` — and with it `converged` — is false. `config_notes`
already reported the move; what it never reported was the consequence, and the consequence is
the part anyone acts on.

**This reports the hole rather than closing it.** The payload still carries one head field
serving two consumers that want opposite ends of a mid-round push: provenance wants the later
commit so a fix pass is not blamed for commits it did not write, and scope wants the head that
was actually reviewed. Closing the gap means recording those apart. A veto is enough while a
human reads the verdict before merging; the day `converged` becomes an input to an automatic
merge gate, the gate would be approving code no round read.

A whole-PR round is deliberately left alone: its next round re-reads the PR, so the range
closes on its own, and its own material came off `gh pr diff` at a moment nothing can date —
so a veto there would fire on rounds that did read the later commit.

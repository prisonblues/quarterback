# the round's churn budget is counted by something that is not the fixer

Every bound on a fix pass is measured from outside it except the one that bounds
how much it may spend. `max_fix_growth` is applied by the caller,
`unrefereed_fix` and `guard_churn` are read off the fix range's own split,
`fix_injection` off provenance — and `low_severity_fix_lines` was measured by
the fix pass. The dial is resolved by the panel, relayed into the fixer's brief
as a paragraph asking it to run `git diff --numstat` after each fix and stop
when the budget is gone, and read by nobody else.

On `prisonblues/lexray#1780` the relayed number was correct at every round and
the passes came out at 850, 322, 356 and 142 added lines against a budget of 40.
Nothing recorded that. `harness_rules.py` already says the principle beside the
dial — the fixer "is never asked 'does this risk ballooning?', because that is a
judgement by the actor whose judgement the 85% impugns" — and the counting had
been handed to that same actor.

### `round_stop.fix_budget`

The round now prices the pass itself, off the split `unrefereed_fix` and
`guard_churn` already read: production at 1, test and prose at
`unrefereed_line_weight`, over churn — the same arithmetic the brief states, so
the reader and the actor are held to one number rather than to two copies of it.
It is published on every round and printed under **Budget spend of the last fix
pass** where a budget was actually in force. The brief now tells the fixer that
this happens, because a measurement the measured party does not know about is a
trap and the point of this is a brake.

### `within` is one-sided, and the payload says which side

The budget bounds the 💸 band and not the pass, and a diff cannot say which line
paid for which finding. So the priced total is an upper bound on the budgeted
spend and only one of its readings is a fact:

- `true` — the whole pass, mandatory work included, priced under the budget. The
  budgeted part is under it too, whatever anybody counted.
- `false` — the budget **cannot be shown** to have been kept. Not a breach: a
  round clearing two P1s may spend three hundred production lines the budget
  never applied to.
- `null` — nothing measured: round 1, an unreadable fix range, no budget
  written, or a repo whose floors meet so there is no band to pay for.

### `breach` is the side `within` does not have

`within: false` was never a violation, so there was no input at all for which
this reported one. A pass whose entire **To fix** list was budgeted P3/P4
findings, priced at 41 production lines against `limit: 40`, ran a round
identical to a four-line pass.

That was the honest shape while the attribution was missing. It is not the
honest shape now: the prior round's **To fix** list and the dials it was banded
under both travel on the baseline payload, the same way `escalated`,
`acknowledged`, `declined` and `retracted` already do. So the round reads them
back, and where **every** finding the fixer was sent to answer sat in the 💸
band there was no mandatory work for the spend to belong to — the priced total
is not an upper bound on the budgeted spend, it *is* the budgeted spend.

- `true` — the whole brief was budgeted and the pass priced over the budget.
  Provable from the diff, the prior round's list and the dials both rounds ran
  under, with nothing taken on the fixer's word. It ends the round, files a veto
  line and costs the round its confidence.
- `false` — the premise held and the pass stayed inside its budget. Stronger
  than `within: true`: this one says the budget was kept by a pass that had no
  mandatory work to hide behind.
- `null` — no strict verdict was available, which is the ordinary case and stays
  ordinary. `brief` carries which of the reasons it was, in a sentence.

The premise is refused rather than guessed at wherever it cannot be established:
an empty prior list (`all()` over nothing is vacuously true), an anchor that is
not the round immediately before this one, a payload whose band floors are not
readable severities, a budget or weight that moved between the two rounds — a
breach claimed against a number the fixer was never given would be about a
policy nobody applied — or **any entry in the prior list this round cannot
read**, whether its severity will not parse or it is not a record at all. An
unreadable finding declines the brief; it never disappears from it, because a
list of one P3 and one malformed entry with the malformed one dropped reads as
wholly budgeted and would have a pass accused of overspending on work nobody
can identify.

### the budget compared is the one APPLIED, not the one written

#551 put a second input under this number. The budget in the fixer's brief is
`min(low_severity_fix_lines, its pro-rata share of the cycle's first round)`,
clamped — so the dial and the number actually spent against are two different
values, and two rounds of one cycle can apply different budgets with nobody
touching a dial (round 1 takes that denominator from its own diff length, round
2 from round 1's recorded `pr_chars`).

The premise therefore compares **applied** budgets, read back out of the anchor
round's own `round_stop.fix_budget`. Compared on the dial instead, a pair of
rounds that spent against 13 and 15 lines would both read `40` and pass, and the
pass would be priced against a bound it was never given — a false breach, and
#551's note puts that divergence on small PRs, which is exactly where the priced
total sits closest to the limit. The written dial is still read for the one
thing it still decides: which findings were in the band at all.

### where it fires, and where it does not

The rung needs the round to be going again, and a round whose prior To fix list
was wholly budgeted is normally a round rules 1-3 already stop. So this
enforces an **exceptional lifecycle** rather than the ordinary budgeted-fix
path: the routes in are rule 2's Sonar hard-gate exemption, which keeps a cycle
going at any severity, and a cycle a caller continued by hand. That is stated
rather than fixed — widening it would mean loosening `going_again`, and that
bound is what stops a spend statistic cancelling the repair round for a P1.

### what still gates nothing

The upper bound. `within` is reported exactly as before and moves `stop` in
neither direction — a loud round whose brief carried a P1 ends where it always
ended. Only `breach: true` reaches the stop rule, it takes the same two bounds
every rung on that chain takes (it can only turn a `go again` into a stop, and
never the round a held-over P1 or a repeat was buying), and there is still no
flag to arm and no new dial: the limit is one the repo already wrote, the band
is one its floors already carve out, and the premise is a proof rather than a
threshold.

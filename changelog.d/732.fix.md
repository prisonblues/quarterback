# the drift check stopped at the top level, and eighteen keys were falling through underneath it

`tests/test_payload_key_drift.py` exists so that a key the panel starts sending and ingest does not bind fails the suite on the commit that adds it. It reads the payload's **top level**. `round_stop` is a nested object bound by `StopIn`, and nothing anywhere compared that model's fields against what the panel nests inside it: the producer returns 24 keys, the model declared 6, and the other 18 were discarded in silence by the same `extra="ignore"` the check was written to end.

That is not a hypothetical. `outstanding` — what a cycle left behind and who it was handed to — was sent from #42, bound by nothing, and found months later by a human noticing a number was missing, with the drift check green throughout.

The check now runs over the nested blocks too, and each of the eighteen has been decided once.

### What the board now keeps

`cleared_floor` — the floor the round was required to clear. The disposal counts stored beside it are *split* at this floor and did not carry it, so a reader holding `{"fixable": 2, "below_floor": 11}` could not say what either number meant without re-deriving a panel-side property from three separate dials.

`new_below_trigger_floor` and `repeated_below_trigger_floor` — how many findings this round raised, and how many an earlier round had already raised, that fell under the trigger floor and so bought no round. Counts rather than key lists. Two numbers and not a sum: one says the floor is turning work away at the door, the other says work already inside the cycle never gets done, and a repo whose backlog is all in the second has a different problem from one where it is all in the first.

`stop_rungs` — the eight escalation rungs and the premise brake's state, verbatim, in one column. Each is a measurement beside its own verdict, and which number out of each matters is a calibration nobody has done; storing them whole is what makes that calibration possible without deciding it in advance.

### What it deliberately does not keep

Six of the eighteen are the same value under a second name or a dial already on the row: three disposal buckets published flat as well as nested, the round number the payload sends at the top level anyway, and two settings the stored policy record has held verbatim for releases. Those are now lines on a hand-written list with a reason each — which is the only other way past the check, and is not silence.

### What it is not

Not `extra="forbid"`. A payload is never refused over one field on this path, because refusing it loses the findings, the scorecards and the accounts along with the bad key. What ends a silent drop is a check that goes red in CI on the commit that adds a key, not a gate that fails a producer in the field.

### The check's own blind spots, after a second opinion

An independent codex pass on the PR found that the fix had reproduced the defect it was fixing, one level up, and it was right. Two changes came out of it.

`NESTED_BLOCKS` was hand-written and nothing compared it against the model, so a nested block added to `ReviewIn` and left out of it would have been a tier nobody looks at — which is exactly how `round_stop` went unchecked for a year. The question is now computed (`nested_model_fields()` unwraps every `BaseModel` out of `ReviewIn`'s annotations) while the answer stays written down: each discovered block must appear in `NESTED_BLOCKS` or in `NESTED_SCAN_UNSUPPORTED` with a reason. Discovery cannot go stale; a decision still has to be typed into a diff.

And the nested readers now fail closed. `**spread`, `dict(...)`, `|` and `.update()` are all invisible to an `ast` reader that collects constant keys, so a producer built that way would have reported fewer keys while every assertion here went on passing — a drift check that has silently stopped checking. The real producers use none of them today, so the assertion costs nothing now and names the commit that introduces one. The red/green for it is done to the scanner against mutated producer source, rather than to the set the scanner already returned.

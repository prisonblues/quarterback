# the review budget is spent per round, because that is what a round costs

`review_panel.budget.tokens_per_pr` was a flat lifetime-of-PR ceiling, and the panel's
unit of work is a **round** — `/panel-review-pr` makes round 2 the default and calls it
"not optional", and `max_rounds` went from 2 to 6 on #621 so a cycle could stop on a
decision rather than on a count. A flat total does not bound spend per unit of work; it
bounds *how many units of work a PR is allowed*, which is the decision the round logic
is supposed to make on evidence. So it bound latest and hardest on the round that
mattered most: spent on rounds 1 and 2, which were happening anyway, it refused round 3
— the first round that reads the fixer's own commit. That is what happened on
`lexray#1761`, a three-file config change, where round 3 was refused before a single
seat was dispatched and nothing read the fix commit.

Measured over this board's own 115 recorded review runs: ten PRs reached round 3, and
of the seven of them that recorded any tokens at all, rounds 1 and 2 take a **median
57%** of the PR's entire measured spend (n=7, 0%-89%, where the 0% is a PR whose first
two rounds recorded no tokens at all; the other three are 0/0 and have no share to
take).
On the worst of them a flat total had 11% of itself left for the four rounds
`max_rounds: 6` exists to allow.

### the round is the dial, and the PR total is derived from it

`review_panel.budget.tokens_per_round` is the new primary dial and `tokens_per_pr` is
now derived from it — `tokens_per_round × max_rounds`. Written below that total,
`tokens_per_pr` is refused **by name at resolve time**, before any board call: the two
could always be set to contradict each other, and #483's complaint is that the
contradiction stayed invisible until a late round was refused in front of whoever was
waiting for the review.

The check runs before any seat is dispatched, so the round about to start has spent
nothing of its own to measure. The allowance is therefore **released one round at a
time**: at a round boundary the ceiling in force is
`tokens_per_round × min(rounds already recorded + 1, max_rounds)`, against
`pr_total.tokens`. A round that overspent is refused at the *next* boundary — a round
gone wrong rather than a PR that has had enough attention — while a cycle whose rounds
each stayed inside the allowance can run every round the cap allows. Under a flat total
the whole cycle's worth is available to round 1 in one go, which is the runaway a
ceiling is actually for. Rounds are counted from the board's own rows (`pr_total.runs`,
the same aggregate over the same rows as the tokens) and never from `--round`, which
restarts under `--new-cycle` while `pr_total` has no time bound at all.

Every ceiling still ships `null`, and the size of the allowance is deliberately not
chosen here: setting any key in the block wakes the budget fleet-wide, and the board's
own history cannot state a per-round figure — 72 of its 115 recorded review runs are
the fleet's ordinary four-seat shape and not one of them measured all four seats (62
measured three, five measured two, five measured none). #483 carries the numbers.

### a budget refusal stops telling its reader to rebase the branch

Three preconditions reach the pre-flight through one `gate` parameter — a branch that
cannot merge (#271), the spend ceiling (#55), and a cycle an earlier round already
ended (#617) — and the refusal notice keyed its whole "what to do" block on the first
of them. So a round refused on the spend ceiling was told to rebase its branch, to set
`review_panel.require_mergeable: false`, and to pass `--force`, which the same refusal
says two paragraphs earlier cannot move a fleet ceiling. None of the three applied. The
gate now carries which precondition it is, and the spend refusal names the remedy that
exists: the board dial.

### and the dial picker stops being complete by coincidence

`dial_matches` bounded itself at 40 results by default, which was exactly how many
board dials there were — so the picker's first answer to *"which dials are there"* was
all of them by arithmetic accident. The 41st dial pushed `spawn.max_sessions_fleet` off
the end of a list whose whole claim is completeness, silently. The bound is opt-in now:
the one caller that wants one asks for two, and the picker gets every match in a list
that scrolls.

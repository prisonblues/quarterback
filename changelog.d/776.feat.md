# a later round can be made to cost less than round 1

`max_rounds` is 6 and every round cost the same, so a cycle that had not converged by round 4 had
exactly as much room to generate new work as round 1 did. That is backwards. From round 2 onward
what a round reads *is* the previous round's fix — 128 of 201 new findings across seven PRs were
written by the fix pass immediately before them, 17 of 17 in one round of PR #299 — and a flat
budget pays full price for news the loop wrote itself.

`review_panel.round_budgets.multipliers` is a curve: one multiplier per round, indexed 1-based,
the last entry reused for every round past the end of the list. It ships `[1.0]`, which is flat and
therefore the identity, so a fleet that ignores the key pays nothing for it. `[1.5, 1.0, 0.75,
0.5]` is the shape the issue pins — spend big on round 1, shrink each round after — and it is an
example rather than the default, because nobody has measured it and a curve shipped at a number
nobody chose is a ceiling arriving switched on.

**Three ceilings, and the list is the contract.** The per-round token allowance
(`budget.tokens_per_round`) as released at a round boundary — summed over the rounds released
rather than multiplied, because each round's allowance is now its own and a product would either
re-price rounds already spent or price a late round at round 1's rate. The round's diff ceiling
(`max_diff_chars`), scaled once before it is handed out, so a seat with no ceiling of its own and
the judge inheriting the same number both read a tapered diff from one wiring point. And
`low_severity_fix_lines`, the churn one round may spend on the band between the two floors.

**What it may not touch is a rule, not a list: a multiplier scales what a round may SPEND and never
what a round may CONCLUDE.** Taper a threshold and the same defect is a finding in round 1 and not
in round 5, so the cycle's verdict becomes a function of its round number — a different review, not
a cheaper one. So no severity floor, no `escalate_on` rung, no growth or guard ceiling, not
`max_rounds`, and none of the ceilings counted over a rolling window or a whole PR, where "round
4's multiplier" names nothing. A ceiling a repo wrote for one seat or for the judge also stands:
those exist because a model genuinely cannot take the change, so they are statements about a
model's capacity rather than about a round's budget. A written `0` is never scaled — that is a repo
saying "spend nothing on that band", and tapering it would loosen it. No ceiling stays no ceiling:
a multiplier over `null` is still `null`, since the curve may tighten a ceiling somebody wrote and
may never create one.

**Running out is a named, non-confident stop and never a partial pass.** A tapered ceiling that
floors below 1 is exhaustion rather than a ceiling of nothing, and it arrives as the ordinary
pre-flight refusal — the round is recorded `reviewed: false` with a skip reason and cannot read as
a panel that looked and found nothing. Nothing drops findings to fit and nothing drops a seat and
counts the round as covered; a dry round is the outcome this work is judged on, and a budget able
to manufacture one would make a review look cleaner the less of it ran. Where the taper is what
bound, the refusal says so and quotes both numbers and the multiplier between them, because a
reader sent to `.harness-rules` for the figure that refused them would not find it there.

**The seat set is the fourth ceiling the issue asks for, and it is refused rather than left
undone.** Dropping a seat is the largest saving available, and the rule for it — scale the size,
floor at 1, never choose which seats — cannot be met today: nothing ranks seats for coverage, so
scaling the size means choosing by alphabet, which is choosing which seats by the one property
unrelated to what they find. And a dropped seat is not a cheaper round, it is a narrower one
counted as coverage — an installed seat with no record, which the round after inherits as quiet.
What is left of the intent is real and already applied: a later round reads less of the PR through
every seat's eyes rather than none of it through one seat's.

**On a stock fleet this reaches one ceiling.** `budget.tokens_per_round` and `max_diff_chars` are
both unset by default, and a multiplier over no ceiling is still no ceiling, so a repo opting in
today gets the taper on its low-severity budget and gets it on the other two the day somebody
writes those numbers. That is the half of #483 still owed: the per-round mechanism landed, the
number did not.

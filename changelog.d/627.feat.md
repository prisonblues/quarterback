# a sub-floor fix that causes findings is excised rather than repaired

`escalate_on.fix_injection` ends a cycle whose fix pass generated more than half
of the round's work, and `round_stop.revert` prices undoing that pass and hands
the decision to a human. That refusal to act stands, and the reason is stated
where it is made: a fix pass is **mixed**, so reverting one that cleared three
P2s to remove five P3s puts the P2s back, and nothing in the loop can tell which
half is which without asking.

**A single fix that answered a finding below `round_trigger_floor` is not a
mixed pass.** It answered one complaint that was, by definition, not blocking the
close, so the whole cost of removing it is one P3 or P4 going back on the board
unfixed — the state every unpaid budget item is already in. There is nothing to
weigh, so there is no decision to take upstairs. Rich's decision on #621, in his
words: *"if a fix of a P3 or P4 itself causes more bugs, we should quickly excise
it, and then be happy to keep going."*

### `round_stop.excision`

Recorded on every round. `excise[]` names one fix apiece — the commit, the
`git revert --no-commit <sha>` for it, the sub-floor finding that comes back
unfixed, and the findings that go away with it — and `declined[]` names a seam it
refused with the sentence saying why. `count` is `null` where nobody could look
(round 1, a rebased range, an unreadable anchor payload, a checkout that does not
carry the pass) and a measured `0` otherwise, which is the count #627 asks for:
a repo where this fires often is saying something about its sub-floor budget that
no other number says.

The findings an excision takes away with it are listed under **Excised, not
fixed** in the report and flagged `excised: true` in the payload, out of the
**To fix** list — there is nothing there for a fixer to do, and repairing the fix
instead is the round the rule exists to skip.

### attribution at the grain of the fix, not the pass

`_provenance` attributes a finding to the fix **pass**: its added-line set is the
whole range's, so a finding standing on one commit's line is indistinguishable
from one standing on another's. `git blame` of the finding's own line at this
round's head answers the finer question, and answers it in the head's line
numbers — which a per-commit diff cannot, because every later commit in the pass
renumbers its post-image. The fixer's half is one instruction in `review-pr.md`:
land each budgeted fix as its own commit and put the finding's id in the body, as
the **To fix** list prints it.

### the revert cannot cascade, and a declined excision says so

A sub-floor fix whose lines a later blocking fix built on is not a clean excision
— taking it out takes part of a P1's fix with it, which is the mixed revert this
rule is careful not to be. The test is that every line the commit added is still
the commit's own at the head: fewer means a later commit rewrote part of it, more
means blame credits it with lines its own diff did not count, and either way the
excision is declined with both numbers in the sentence and the caused finding
stays in the cycle.

### the excision's own restoration is churn, and it is counted

#692's unit is churned lines — a line that is touched is surface area for bugs,
cumulative across passes — and an excision touches lines: it deletes what the
sub-floor fix wrote and restores what that fix replaced. None of it is
exempted. The excision commit lands inside the next round's fix range, so the
churn split, the guard ceiling, the budget pricing and the surface count all see
it like any other commit.

What #559 exempts is **attribution**, which is the other question: restoring
already-reviewed code counts as writing it to a line diff, so the lines an
excision puts back — which sat at an earlier round's head by construction — come
out of `introduced` rather than inflating the gate the excision is answering.
Below `RESTORED_RUN_MIN` (five consecutive lines) a restoration is not
distinguished from authorship, and that exposure is stated rather than closed.

`low_severity_fix_lines` is one of those readings and the excision is charged to
it like any other commit: `fix_budget_state` prices the referee's split, the
revert commit is in that split, and nothing exempts it. Exempting it would mean
finding the revert inside the next round's fix range and subtracting its churn,
and #692 settled the unit as churned lines so that no correction gets to be free.
The exposure is stated rather than closed — where the rest of the anchor round's
brief was wholly budgeted, a large excision can fire an overspend against a pass
that only did what the rule ordered.

### what it deliberately does not do

It moves no verdict. The caused finding is still outstanding to every rule in
`round_stop`, so a round naming an excision goes again exactly as it would have
without one — the cycle continues, which is the whole point of the rule being
cheap. A Sonar hard-gate issue is never excised, because it is exempt from both
severity floors and this rule's argument is that nothing above the floor was
owed.

And it does not price what the excision destroys. `destroys` names the files, the
lines and how many of them sit in test or documentation paths; #558 is open on
the fact that a revert proposal prices what comes back and never what goes away,
and a sub-floor fix is very often the only test over the path it was written for.
The gap is named in the payload, the report and the orchestrator's brief rather
than inherited quietly. Selective replacement of a genuinely mixed pass still
needs #623's typed findings: "keep the repairs that answered a regression" is not
a distinction `git` can make.

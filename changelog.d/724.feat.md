# the reviewer brief had a dimension for load-bearing tests and none for load-bearing prose

Measured on the three PRs landed 2026-09-03 (#720, #719, #715). Each had already been through a review pass, each was green, and each got one more independent adversarial pass before landing. **Two of the three defects that pass found were in the prose**, and the third was a prose claim that happened to be true.

`REVIEW_PROMPT` already carried the analogous dimension for tests — a test that is PRESENT but would not have failed against the defect it names — and it is one of the sharpest bullets in the brief. The only documentation dimension beside it was **staleness**: "behaviour changes that leave CLAUDE.md, docs, README, or docstrings stale". That catches a comment the diff LEFT BEHIND. It has nothing to say about one the diff WROTE, asserting a property of the code beside it that the code does not have.

#719's `qb-reconcile` comment said both facts "are re-asked at the point of the WRITE rather than inferred from the finding", and "the write asks GitHub's own word for it". Read plainly that is a fresh `gh` call immediately before mutating the plan, a second safety boundary. There is none — `apply_candidates` reads `finding.evidence["github_state"]`, populated during the earlier pass. The intent was defensible and `MERGED` is terminal, so nothing was actually unsafe; the comment described a guard that does not exist, in the file whose entire thesis is that a check nobody made must not read as a check that passed. #715's `panel_seats.PR_HOLD_TTL` comment had its comparison backwards and was corrected by an earlier round of its own review.

### the dimension

A comment or docstring the diff writes that states a checkable property of the code — "this is the only caller", "nothing between here and there returns", "this re-reads X rather than trusting the earlier read", "this cannot be reached" — is a claim, and a claim in the diff is reviewable. Check the ones the change leans on.

It sits immediately after Documentation, which puts the list in pairs: `Test coverage` then `Load-bearing tests`, `Documentation` then `Load-bearing comments`. Each pair is "absent" then "present and wrong", and that is the shape a reviewer works through rather than an ordering.

**Priced by consequence, and the rule is stated in both places.** In most codebases a wrong comment is a nit. Here the comments are the design record — a fifty-line docstring carrying the argument, the measurement and the rejected alternative is the normal unit — so a wrong one propagates: the next agent reads it, believes it, and builds on it. And it is worse than a wrong test in one specific way, which is the sentence the bullet has to carry: a wrong test can at least go red, and nothing ever EXECUTES a comment, so a wrong one survives every round, every CI run and every rebase. The rule is in the bullet and again in the severity paragraph, because the dimension list says what to look for and the severity paragraph says what it is worth — and a seat reaching for a P-number reads the second.

**The severity is conditioned on consequence, not on the artefact being a comment.** #724 argued a blanket "at least P2" and the first cut of this shipped one. It is too coarse, and severity is what the fix pass acts on: a floor covering every false checkable claim — a stale count, a wrong complexity description, a local detail nothing rests on — turns documentation nits into mandatory repairs and lengthens rounds. The discriminator is what relying on the claim would cost. A claim the change's own correctness argument leans on (a guard's justification, an ordering or concurrency property, the reason no `try/finally` was needed) is **P2** when false; a claim nothing depends on is **P3**, priced like any other documentation defect. Both tiers name it, so the scale carries the same rule as the dimension.

`/panel`'s own summary of the dimensions it sends and `/review-pr`'s Completeness list carry it too. The panel and the single reviewer apply the same bar by design, so a dimension in one and not the other means which reviewer you spent decides whether the comments in the diff were read at all — and `panel.md`'s list is what a reader consults when deciding whether to spend a panel.

**Parity is both halves, and the first cut only carried one.** `REVIEW_PROMPT` routes a claim the material cannot settle into `could_not_assess`, a channel its reply envelope already has. `/review-pr` has no envelope — it returns a table — so carrying the check across without a place to put the residue left that workflow asking for something it could not report, under a PR body claiming parity. Its report format gains an `Unverified claims` line, written out with `none` for `Surface`'s reason: a missing section reads as forgotten. It matters beyond tidiness, because that residue is exactly the population the deferred half is to be measured on, and a workflow that cannot record it cannot contribute to the count.

### expensive is not impossible, and the bullet has to say which

#715's `panel.run` carried "there is no early return between the claim and here — the skip and refusal exits are all ABOVE the dispatch". That one is TRUE, and confirming it is the point: the span is ~2,600 lines and the claim is what justifies shipping without a `try/finally`. What settled it here was an AST walk over `run`'s body enumerating the returns and checking each was inside a nested `def` — **convenience, not necessity**. Grep `return` over the span, read the enclosing scopes, and you reach the same answer by hand.

The first cut of this changelog said such a claim was "not settleable from the seat's checkout", which is false and false in the direction that costs something. `CODE_ACCESS_BRIEF` gives a code-reading seat Read, Grep and Glob and **no shell**, deliberately (#458, #459); that makes a structural question laborious, not unanswerable. And `CODE_ACCESS_BRIEF`'s own rule is that a question you can answer by opening a file is *not* a coverage gap, while a declared gap costs the round its confident stop (`coverage_veto`) — so a dimension telling seats these questions are unsettleable would manufacture exactly those declarations and pay a real price to record a check nobody attempted.

So the bullet says to grep the callers and read the enclosing scopes FIRST, and reserves `could_not_assess` for what the material genuinely cannot settle *after* looking. A load-bearing claim nobody actually checked is not better than a false one, just quieter; a claim declared unassessable without anyone opening a file is worse than both, because it reads as the check having been attempted.

### the second half is deliberately not built

#724's other proposal is to close that gap — give the seat a shell, or a canned set of structural queries it can request. Not here, and the reason is the issue's own: it asks for a measurement first, counting how many `could_not_assess` entries and unverified comment claims are structural questions a script would settle, the same way #716 was measured. That count does not exist yet, and the `could_not_assess` entries this dimension produces are how it gets taken. Undoing "you have NO shell" ahead of it would spend a constraint's own argument (#458, #459) to buy an answer nobody has.

### and the shape guard needed rewiring rather than relaxing

`test_the_review_prompt_is_still_one_bullet_per_dimension` counted bullets containing "Load-bearing" and asserted there was exactly one. With two such dimensions that assertion is a claim that they have not both arrived — the opposite of what the test is for — so it counts per NAMED dimension now. The assertions around it fold a bullet's continuation lines onto its opener before searching: the comments dimension talks about tests and the tests dimension about assertions, and an unfolded grep for "claim" beside "comment" is answered by two adjacent bullets that each say one of them.

### what an independent pass found, on a change about reviewing prose

Four findings, and two of them were false claims in this change's own prose — on the PR that teaches a panel to review the comments a diff writes. That is not an argument against the dimension; it is the strongest evidence for it, and it is the second time this diff made the case that way: the author's own pass over it, before review, had already found four more in the test helpers.

- **The blanket floor**, above. Assigned by artefact type rather than by consequence.
- **"Not settleable from the seat's checkout"**, above. False, and false in the expensive direction.
- **The parity that did not exist**, above.
- **The parity tests searched the whole file.** They would have stayed green with the instruction deleted from the operative section while the words survived in prose elsewhere — and this change made that concrete rather than hypothetical, because the `Unverified claims` block it adds to the report format matches two of the three patterns the Completeness assertions use. They delimit their sections now, the way `_dimensions` already did for `REVIEW_PROMPT`, and they assert both halves: the check, the severity rule, and the instruction to look before declaring.

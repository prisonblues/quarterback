# the threshold that ends review cycles was measured, and the answer is that it cannot be yet

`escalate_on.fix_injection` ends a review cycle when more than half a round's new
outstanding findings were attributed to the fix pass before it. Since `max_rounds`
went from 2 to 6 (#621, PR #631) it is **the** thing that ends a cycle early — and
a cycle it ends ends on an escalation rather than a decision, which is the outcome
the convergence epic exists to stop. Its `0.5` was fitted to rounds whose
outstanding findings were P3/P4-heavy, and #623, #615 and the floors all moved
underneath it in the same commit that raised the cap.

The instruction to re-measure lived in two comments and was tracked nowhere.
`harness_rules.py` said, in capitals, *treat the first cycles under `max_rounds: 6`
as the recalibration and expect this number to move*. This is that measurement.

### The population is one round

Taken 2026-09-02 over the whole board, all time:

* **One attributable round exists since the cap moved.** `f53bdcd` (2026-08-31) is
  where `max_rounds: 6`, #623's blocking/observation split and #615's `narrowed`
  clearing all landed together. lexray#1813 round 2 is the only round recorded
  since with anything to attribute: 13 new outstanding findings, 6 of them
  attributed, rate **0.462** — under the threshold, so it did not fire. It is also
  the only round on the board whose `rules` record says what the dial was set to
  while it ran, so the other 37 rated rounds cannot be held against any threshold
  at all.
* **No false-positive rate is computable, because there are no healthy cycles.**
  27 cycles carry an attributable round and `converged` is TRUE on none of them —
  one false, 26 never measured; fleet-wide the board reports 42 cycles and
  `decided: 1`. A false positive is this rung stopping a cycle that was
  converging, and the population holds no converging cycle for it to have stopped.
  The number that decides the trade has no denominator rather than a large one.
* **The old population is one-sided.** 38 rated rounds, every one at or above
  `FIX_INJECTION_MIN_NEW`: min 0.000, p25 0.460, median 0.696, p75 0.876, max
  1.000, and 28 of 38 over 0.5. Swept, the firing share runs 86.8% at 0.3, 73.7%
  at 0.5, 47.4% at 0.7 and 13.2% at 0.9 — monotone, no elbow, no second mode. Ten
  rounds fall at or below 0.5, putting the threshold near the 26th percentile of
  what has been observed.
* **And it is structurally silent about a cap of 6.** 20 of the 27 cycles cross the
  threshold and every one first crosses at round 2 — the round the cap of 2 then
  in force was ending anyway — with 15 of the 20 having no round 3 recorded. Five
  cycles forgo a round at all. Three quarters of the evidence for this number was
  gathered where firing it changed nothing but the `reason` string.
* **#559's filter has not yet moved a recorded number.** It landed the same day and
  `provenance_restored` is absent on all 38 rounds: correctly so on a round 2,
  whose only prior round *is* the anchor, and no round 3 or later has been recorded
  since. The instrument #637 rightly insisted on fixing first is fixed and
  unexercised.

**So `0.5` stands, as a decision not yet taken rather than as a validation.**
Moving it on one round fits a threshold to n=1; fitting it to the older population
fits it to rounds gathered where firing was free. The block now records the
measurement, its date, the query that produced it and what it could not settle,
instead of asking for something that had already been done.

### `injection_by_round` on `GET /review/convergence`

The reason the instruction sat unanswered is that the quantity was not published.
`marginal_by_round` counts findings; this rule *divides* them —
`provenance_counts.introduced` over the sum of all four buckets, per **round** —
and `/review/stats` publishes a ratio of sums across a window per reviewer, which
is a different number: a pooled ratio weights a 44-finding round like a 4-finding
one and the rule does not. So a recalibration had to pull `/reviews` and do the
arithmetic by hand.

Per round number: `rounds`, `attributed_runs` (the panel sent a tally),
`rated_runs` (and the denominator was non-zero), the two sums with `pooled_rate`,
and the shape the rule actually reads —
`rate_min`/`rate_p25`/`rate_median`/`rate_p75`/`rate_max`. Beside them `dial_runs`
counts the rated rounds carrying a non-empty `rules` record, the only field that
says what the threshold *was*: a threshold fitted across rounds that cannot name
their own is fitted across a denominator that moved underneath it.

**No threshold and no minimum denominator is applied**, and both omissions are the
point. The dial belongs to a repo and `FIX_INJECTION_MIN_NEW` to the harness, so
the board publishes the population and never the verdict — the same line
`app/api/dials.py` draws and `m6bc45ff1` draws for `converged`.

### The founding measurement, re-derived

`128 of 201 new findings across seven PRs — 63.7%` is quoted in a dozen places and
named no PR; the board holds fourteen PRs panelled on 2026-08-16. It reproduces
exactly from the stored rounds and picks out one subset of seven: quarterback
**#151, #152, #153, #154, #158, #160 and #161**, last round of each. Those PR
numbers are now written down beside the figure, because a calibration citing a
population nobody can enumerate is one rebase from #692's failure mode.

39 of 53 after round 1 on PR #299 (17 of 17 in its round 2) and 64% then 87% on
the #489 cycle (lexray#1709 rounds 2-3, 9/14 and 13/15) both reproduce too. One
sentence did not: *"every one of those is far above 0.5"* is true of the three
quoted aggregates and false of the rounds behind the first, which run 0.321,
0.513, 0.541, 0.694, 0.824, 0.889 and 0.923 — one of the seven sits well under the
threshold its own pooled figure justifies. It now claims only what it can support.
